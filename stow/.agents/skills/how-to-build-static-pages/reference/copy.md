# Copy rules

Write new copy for landing, home, and product pages. Use the tone and terms from BRANDING.md. Do not copy the sample article.

## Set the message

- Give the page one audience, one idea, one offer, and one primary action.
- Write for one reader with one problem. Give one unique value. Use separate pages for separate audiences.
- Read reviews, support tickets, and community posts. Use the reader's words for the problem.
- Select the reader's awareness stage: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, or most aware.
- Match the headline and proof to that stage. A most-aware reader needs the price and offer. A problem-aware reader needs the problem.
- Match the promise in the ad, email, or link that leads to the page.
- Remove actions that compete with the primary action.
- Add a weaker secondary action only for readers who are less ready to act.

## Build the content

- Use this order: headline, subheadline, main proof, benefits, product steps, testimonial, answers to concerns, and final action.
- Answer concerns about price, time, privacy, and cancellation.
- Give each section one job. Remove repeated points.

## Write the headline and subheadline

- Start with a specific result for the reader. Aim for 6 to 12 words in the headline.
- Use Cut reporting time in half instead of Work smarter.
- Do not use Welcome, Our Services, or a slogan without meaning.
- Use a pun only if the brand is playful and the meaning stays clear.
- Add audience, method, or proof in the subheadline. Use one or two sentences. Do not repeat the headline.

## Explain the benefits

- State what the reader can do with each feature. Explain why the result matters.
- Put the result before the feature. Do not list a feature without its benefit.
- Use See where users drop off instead of Gain comprehensive insights into user behavior.

## Make the text easy to scan

- Use one idea per sentence. Use one to three sentences per paragraph.
- Use short, common words. Put the important words first.
- Give each section heading a point, not only a label.
- Use lists of three to five items. Do not nest lists.

## Show the action and proof

- Put the primary action near the top. Repeat it after a long section and at the end.
- Give the action label a result and the effort required. Example: Start my free trial.
- Do not use Submit, Learn more, or Click here alone.
- Use real numbers, customers, logos, or quotes near the decision point.
- Give each testimonial a name, role, and result.
- Do not invent proof. Remove claims without proof. Do not use vague claims such as trusted by many.
- Put trial, guarantee, cancellation, or privacy terms near the action. Do not put them only in the footer.
- Add reassurance when the risk is real. Example: No card required.
- Keep the privacy policy easy to find.

## Keep one voice

- Use the same product name, terms, spelling, and action labels across all pages.
- Use the same tone in headings, buttons, forms, and errors.
- Use the active voice. Speak to the reader as you.
- Do not use jargon, hype, emojis, or an em dash. Do not use exclamation marks in headings.
- Remove words, images, and links that do not support the primary action.
- Keep global navigation on home and content pages only. Do not add it to campaign landing pages.
- Read the copy aloud. Change sentences that sound like a template.
